The problem

Customers find out they were scammed after the money is gone.

Mobile money scams use fake MTN messages, impersonation, fraudulent promotions, phishing links and social engineering. Today, protection mostly depends on customers recognising the scam themselves.

Fake MTN messages

Lookalike sender names and fake “payment received” alerts are built to look exactly like real MoMo receipts.

Impersonation and pressure

“Customer care agents” threaten to block a wallet unless the customer shares a PIN or calls a personal number right away.

Promotions and phishing

Fake prizes, loans and free bundles ask for a “processing fee” or send people to fake MTN websites that steal their PIN.

How it works

Protection that starts in the inbox.

FraudX steps in where most scams begin, which is the customer’s messages. It warns them before they act, not after they report a loss.

A message arrives

On Android, FraudX sees every new SMS the moment it lands. On any phone, customers can paste a message they are unsure about.

AI checks it on the phone

More than 20 fraud signals, covering the sender, links, PIN requests and scam stories, are combined with an AI language model. The result is a 0–100 risk score. Nothing is uploaded.

The customer is warned in plain language

A risky message triggers an instant alert that says what the scam is, why FraudX flagged it and what to do. Links in it are disabled, and one tap reports it to MTN.

The detection engine

Fraud rules you can explain, plus AI that reads the language.

Rules make every warning explainable. The language model catches scams phrased in ways the rules don’t cover yet. Together they give one risk score.

65%

Fraud rules

  • Sender: official ID, lookalike, or personal number claiming to be MTN
  • Links: fake MTN domains, link shorteners, raw IP addresses
  • PIN/OTP requests and payment-approval tricks
  • Refund, prize-fee, threat and emergency stories
+
35%

AI language model

A Naive Bayes model on words and word pairs. It runs entirely on the phone and names the words that made a message look suspicious.

Risk score 0–100

Safety floors apply: any PIN request scores at least 85, even from a trusted contact. A fake MTN website scores at least 72.
